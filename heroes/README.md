# Hero archetypes

Real screenshots (desktop, Nov 2026) sorted by composition. Look at the images in a folder before you choose it; the file names say what to notice. **Pick the archetype from the client's strongest real asset** (a great job photo, a strong logo mark, a recognisable van, the owner, a clear before/after, a service that suits a diagram), and pick one the last few SarniaSites builds did not use (see `../guides/house-fingerprint.md`).

Risk = how easily it turns into a template. High-risk archetypes are allowed only when the shape or motif comes from this client's trade or brand.

## Notes on what makes each one work

- **Every hero here is a composition of several pieces**, never one boxed photo with a drop shadow next to the headline.
- **The motif comes from the business.** Thunderbuns' zig-zags come from the word "thunder"; Klindworth's photo cuts come from its logo; WAW's footprints come from walking; Pure Reflections' haze-clearing loop is the service itself.
- **Type is confident.** Most of these headlines are 5–12× body size with tight leading. Weak scale contrast is the most common tell in our own drafts.
- **There is one primary action.** Lune has one button, Graza has one, Haven has a quiet "Let's talk". Our drafts need Call + Get a quote, but make one of them dominant.
- **Breaks on mobile when:** text sits over a busy photo without a scrim (02, 12), a mask leaves only slivers of the photo (03), the marquee is too fast (10), the illustration shrinks to nothing (07). Recompose for 390px; don't just scale down.

## Anti-pattern reminders

The `../anti-examples/` folder shows the defaults: a Webflow trade template (stock worker cut-out in a teal circle, yellow rings, "500+ satisfied clients" stat strip), a cluttered real plumber site (centred title over dark video, coupon box, floating side tab), and our own first Datum draft (eyebrow + headline + boxed photo on a grid, then three identical cards). If a draft could swap logos with any of these, it fails.
